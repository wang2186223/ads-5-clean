“Rowan, why didn’t we watch a different movie?” Jenny complained. She wasn’t a fan of comedies, especially ones that didn’t feature any of her favorite attractive actors or actresses.

When Rowan mentioned going to a movie, she thought he meant a romantic drama or a lighthearted love story–not this.

Jenny was a little displeased.

Rowan frowned, “Out of all the options, this one seemed the most interesting. The others were mostly kids‘ movies, and I’m not into that. If you don’t like it, you can just use your phone for a while.”

“I didn’t mean I didn’t like it,” Jenny quickly backtracked, sensing his annoyance.

Fine, it wasn’t unbearable. She could deal with it.

Her gaze drifted toward the front of the theater, where two women were sitting.

The fact that only four people were in the room made it clear this movie wasn’t exactly popular.

Whatever.

Rowan had been in a strange mood these past few days, so it was better not to push him too much. Jenny absentmindedly munched on her popcorn, though she found it tasteless. This theater didn’t even make good snacks, and the movie wasn’t her kind of thing either.

Romantic movies were much better for couples. She didn’t understand why Rowan had picked a comedy. In romantic films, couples always held hands, cuddled, or shared sweet kisses as the plot

unfolded.

Jenny felt like they were missing an opportunity to connect.

Lately, it didn’t even feel like they were a real couple. They hardly kissed anymore.

She found herself more and more distracted, barely paying attention to the film.

Up ahead, the two women were laughing heartily at a scene.

Rowan, who had been watching the movie, suddenly started staring at one of the women instead of the

screen.

complaining, maybe they could enjoy the film together too.

If he didn’t want to hear her talk, then she’d give him what he wanted.
