His calmness made Hadley uneasy. “You’re not concerned?”

“I am,” he admitted after a pause. “But worrying won’t solve anything. Life goes on.”

His eyes softened as he glanced toward Joy, her cheeks stained from the food. “Joy, how’s your breakfast?”

“Mmm! So yummy!”

Hadley shook her head with a faint smile. He clearly had his own reasoning. No point in pushing it.

“By the way,” Eric said, pointing toward the window, “last night—what were you doing standing there?”

Hadley looked up quickly. So he had seen her. She hadn’t imagined it. Eric chuckled, already knowing.

“You were putting up those decorations, right? They look great.” Then he asked, “Any plans for Christmas?”

Plans?

Hadley hesitated and then glanced at Joy. “Nothing special. Just staying in with Joy.”

It was their usual way of spending the holiday.

This year, with Melba around, it’d be a bit livelier—Melba’s daughter wasn’t coming home from abroad.

Joy tugged on Eric’s sleeve. “Will you stay with me too?”

Children had no filter.

“Joy,” Hadley chided gently, frowning at her. “You can’t say that. He has to spend Christmas with his own family.”

“Oh…” Joy pouted but nodded reluctantly.
